Google Pay is a digital wallet that lets consumers pay quickly and securely without entering card details on every purchase. It uses tokenized credentials and Google account authentication to reduce friction while protecting payment data. {{ className: 'lead' }}

## Benefits of Google Pay

- **Simplified experience**: Pay in one or few steps using cards already stored in the customer’s Google account.
- **Enhanced security**: Uses network tokens instead of the full PAN whenever available, reducing exposure of sensitive data.
- **Broad compatibility**: Works on Android and the web with eligible Visa, Mastercard, Amex, and Diners Club / Discover cards.
- **Strong authentication**: Leverages Google authentication and supports security validation (OTP) when required by the issuer.
- **Multi-platform integration**: Enable Google Pay in Webcheckout and flows like microsites or payment links to keep a consistent experience.

## Supported card networks {{ id: 'supported-networks' }}

- **Visa, Mastercard, and Amex**: Processed via network token (DPAN) or PAN based on issuer support.
- **Diners Club and Discover**: Diners Club operates under the **Discover** network in Google Pay API. Webcheckout handles network mapping automatically and prompts for OTP if required by the issuer.

## How to integrate Google Pay in your business

1. **Set up Google Pay on your Placetopay account**: Contact the operations team to enable this feature on your site.
2. **Access the integration**: Google Pay is available in Webcheckout and options like microsites and payment links.
3. **Flexible processing**:
   - Process transactions using the network token provided by Google Pay when applicable.
   - If the issuer does not support tokenization, you can process with the PAN provided by Google Pay under the corresponding policies.
4. **Additional benefit**: If your customers already have cards saved in Google Pay, they can pay without re-entering data, boosting conversion.

## Google Pay in iframes and WebViews {{ id: 'embedded-and-webview' }}

If Webcheckout is opened inside an iframe or a WebView, that context must meet additional conditions that depend on your integration:

| Environment               | What the merchant must configure                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| :------------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| iframe on your website    | Add the `allow="payment"` attribute to the iframe that loads Webcheckout. If there are nested iframes, each one must include it, and if the iframe does not set `src` (dynamically created content), use `allow="payment *"`. Also see [Digital wallets in iframes](/en/checkout/lightbox#wallets-in-iframes). |
| Android WebView           | Enable the Payment Request API in the WebView and declare the Google Pay queries in your app. The device must have Google Play services 25.18.30 or higher and Android System WebView 137 or higher. See the [step-by-step configuration](/en/checkout/integration/mobile#google-pay-android-webview).         |
| iOS WebView (`WKWebView`) | Google documents a configuration for `WKWebView`, but its FAQ states that the availability check always returns `false` in iOS WebViews. See the [iOS considerations](/en/checkout/integration/mobile#wallets-ios-webview).                                                                                    |

### Availability and payment errors {{ id: 'availability-and-payment-errors' }}

The availability check and the payment flow are separate stages:

- **Availability check**: Webcheckout queries Google with `isReadyToPay` and only displays the button if the response is positive. Google documents that this check returns `false` when the Android device does not meet the Google Play services and Android System WebView version requirements, and that it always returns `false` in iOS WebViews.
- **Payment errors**: a positive `isReadyToPay` response does not guarantee that the payment will be completed. In our tests with an Android app without the WebView configuration, the Google Pay button was displayed and the flow failed when starting; it worked after applying the [Android configuration](/en/checkout/integration/mobile#google-pay-android-webview). When the flow fails, the Google Pay payment is not completed and the payer can choose another method enabled in the session.

<Note>

## Use cases

Google Pay is ideal for:

- **Mobile customers**: Android or Chrome users who already rely on Google Pay for quick purchases.
- **Authenticated users**: Customers who confirm payments with their account or biometrics, reducing friction.
- **Tokenization options**: Merchants aiming to process with tokens to lower fraud risk.
- **Businesses with multiple channels**: Consistent experience across web, microsites, and payment links where Google Pay is enabled.
- **Digital wallets**: Complements other wallets to offer more choices to buyers.

## Advantages of using Google Pay

- **Reduced cart abandonment**: Fast payments lower friction in Webcheckout.
- **Customer trust**: Google’s brand and user experience increase buyer confidence.
- **Optimized conversion**: Delivers an agile, device-friendly user journey.
- **Dedicated technical support**: Placetopay ensures quick implementation and ongoing support.
